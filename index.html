SMALL MOVES, BETTER MONEY HABITS

A few things worth knowing

Useful reminders as you shape your plan.

↘01

Aim for the band, not just the balance

A lender's 60% LTV tier may matter more than shaving off another tiny amount. Aim a little below the boundary to give yourself breathing room.

◷02

Keep your cash cushion

Overpayments can reduce interest, but money paid into a mortgage is harder to access again. Keep a separate emergency fund for life's surprises.

⌁03

Compare like with like

When the time comes, compare the total cost: rate, fees, incentives, term and flexibility — not only the headline monthly payment.

i

A note on these estimates

This is an educational planning tool, not a mortgage offer or financial advice. It estimates monthly interest at the rate entered and assumes regular payments continue until the fix end date. Lenders may calculate interest daily, apply overpayments differently, use their own valuation and set specific LTV bands. Fees, early-repayment limits and future rates aren't included. Confirm figures and eligibility with your lender or a qualified mortgage adviser before acting.

Projection method: the estimated balance is calculated from the entered starting balance, monthly interest rate and number of payments remaining, minus each regular payment and overpayment. The recommended overpayment solves for the monthly extra needed to reach the target balance without a lump sum. The cash-needed figure shows the full top-up needed before considering cash you have already planned; the explanation beneath it indicates whether that planned amount covers the need.

Timing: payments are assumed on the 1st of each month after the balance date, up to but not including the fixed-rate end date. Check that the balance date reflects when the entered statement balance applies.
